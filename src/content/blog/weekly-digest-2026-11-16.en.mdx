## What I listened to this week

This week (Nov 16–Nov 22) I wrote notes on 28 podcast episodes. Here are the takeaways from each one in one place. If an episode catches your eye, the link under it goes to the full post.

## Asianometry — episode of 2026-11-15

The Urals put the first usable computers into 128 Soviet cities, then got routed around in a standards fight decided by software and interoperability rather than clock speed. Notes from Asianometry's 2026-11-15 episode on the Soviet Ural computers: 183 machines built, 73% availability, 18.5 hours between failures, and the 1966–67 committee that chose to clone the IBM System/360.

## Macro Musings with David Beckworth — episode of 2026-11-16

In the 16 November 2026 episode of Macro Musings, host David Beckworth talks with Robin Wigglesworth, editor of FT Alphaville, about his new book A Fabulous Debt: BIS data put the global bond market at around $188 trillion at the end of 2025, larger than global equities and larger than the banking system. The first tradable bond appeared in Venice in 1171 at 5% a year, the war it financed was lost, the principal was never repaid, and the Doge who issued it was killed by his own citizens.

## Bloomberg Odd Lots — episode of 2026-11-16

Notes on the Odd Lots episode of 16 November 2026 (recorded on the morning of 12 November), where Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway talk with Tian Yang, who runs the VPX Fund at Variant Perception, about his impossible trilemma: sovereign mobilisation, domestic cohesion and sound money are hard to hold together, and historically the leg that gives way is sound money. On the day of recording, Jensen Huang's departure from NVIDIA was confirmed, the stock fell as much as 14% intraday and the market cap dropped below $4 trillion, while the S&P 500 was down 0.17%. Tian Yang hangs the whole argument on one assumption: that a lot more compute still buys a lot smarter a model.

## Dwarkesh Podcast — episode of 2026-11-16

Notes on the Dwarkesh Podcast episode recorded on 16 November 2026 with Paul Christiano. He says the risk of AI takeover or human disempowerment over the next year looks more like 10% than 1%, and that on the current trajectory without a slowdown there is roughly a one-in-three chance of superintelligence within a year. His proposal is that frontier labs pause further efficiency gains in pre-training and reinforcement learning until their security posture holds — months, quite possibly six to twelve. Both the probabilities and the proposal rest on a redacted report and one condition: that recursive self-improvement actually connects.
